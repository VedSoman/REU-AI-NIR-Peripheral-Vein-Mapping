Paper 5

Title : Challenges and Artificial Intelligence Solutions for Clinically Optimal Hepatic Venous Vessel Segmentation

Authors : Håvard Bjørke Jenssen, Varatharajan Nainamalai, Egidijus Pelanis, Rahul P. Kumar and colleagues

Published : 2025

Journal : Biomedical Signal Processing and Control

Publisher : Elsevier

Research Objective :
The goal of this paper is to develop an AI-based hepatic vein segmentation system that accurately extracts liver blood vessels from CT 
images for clinical applications such as Liver surgery planning, Tumor localization, Liver transplantation, Ablation therapy, Clinical 
diagnosis.
The authors emphasize that accurate vessel segmentation is essential because liver vessels have complex branching structures and vary 
considerably across patients.

Imaging Technique :
This work uses: Contrast-enhanced Computed Tomography (CT) instead of Near Infrared imaging. The CT scans provide detailed three-
dimensional anatomical information, making them suitable for segmenting hepatic vessels.

Image Preprocessing : 
The authors evaluate -
1. Smoothing Filter
Purpose: Remove image noise, Reduce artifacts, Improve image quality before segmentation.

2. Vesselness Filter
Purpose: Enhance tubular structures, Suppress non-vessel regions, Increase vessel contrast.
This preprocessing helps the neural network focus on vessel structures rather than surrounding tissues.

AI Models : 
The authors compare two segmentation networks:
Dense U-Net
Features:- Dense feature connections, Better feature reuse, Improved gradient flow, Better segmentation of small vessels.

Residual U-Net
Features:- Residual blocks, Easier training of deeper networks, Improved convergence.
After comparison, Dense U-Net combined with preprocessing filters produced the best performance.

Clinical Evaluation :
Unlike many segmentation studies, the authors did not rely only on Dice scores. They also performed clinical assessment, checking whether
important vessel branches required by surgeons were correctly identified.

Dataset Used :
Dataset Type - Combination of:- Public CT datasets, Proprietary (hospital) CT datasets.
Imaging Modality - Contrast-enhanced CT
Annotation - Expert manual vessel segmentation masks.
Purpose - Segment hepatic veins and evaluate clinically important vessel branches.

Performance Metrics :
The study uses - Dice Score, Clinical vessel assessment, Detection of major and minor vessels.
Best Results:
Dense U-Net + preprocessing
Dice score: 0.8144 on the internal dataset
Dice score: 0.7859 on the public dataset

Key Results:
The proposed framework - 
Improved segmentation accuracy with preprocessing.
Dense U-Net outperformed Residual U-Net.
Correctly identified clinically relevant vessels in about 85% of cases.
Demonstrated that preprocessing enhances AI segmentation.

Advantages :
Clinical validation beyond standard metrics
Comparison of multiple AI models
Uses both public and proprietary datasets
Demonstrates the benefit of preprocessing
Dense U-Net improves vessel segmentation
Focuses on clinically relevant vessel structures
Applicable to surgical planning

Limitations :
Focused on hepatic veins, not peripheral veins.
Uses CT imaging rather than NIR imaging.
Segmentation quality decreases for very small or complex vessels.
Some predictions extend beyond true vessel boundaries.
Computational requirements are higher than lightweight edge-AI systems.

Jenssen et al. (2025) investigated AI-based hepatic venous vessel segmentation using Dense U-Net and Residual U-Net architectures 
combined with smoothing and vesselness preprocessing filters. The proposed Dense U-Net achieved Dice scores of 0.8144 on an internal 
dataset and 0.7859 on a public dataset while correctly identifying clinically important vessel structures in approximately 85% of cases. 
The study demonstrates that preprocessing significantly improves segmentation quality. However, the framework is designed for CT-based 
hepatic imaging rather than real-time NIR peripheral vein mapping, highlighting the need for lightweight AI models tailored to portable 
clinical applications.
